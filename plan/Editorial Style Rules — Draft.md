# Editorial Style Rules — Draft

Status: proposal for discussion. This document is not an amendment to
`docs/production-rules.md` or `docs/production-quality-workflow.md`. If there is a
conflict, those authoritative documents win.

September 9, 2026: the owner approved the source-led commentary principles documented
below. They are now reflected in production rules R07, R15, R19, and R32–R34. The remaining
Studio proposal/profile mechanisms in this document are still drafts, not implemented features.

## What belongs where

| Layer | Purpose | Examples | Authority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Universal production rules | Safety, truthfulness, timing integrity, source preservation, required review, accessibility baseline | No fabricated evidence; no clipped speech; inspect actual output; preserve originals | Existing authoritative documents |
| Style profile | Stable but revisable owner preferences for a channel or format | Restrained motion; selective captions; ask before vocal effects | Owner-approved, versioned preference |
| Contextual decision | A proposal for one semantic beat in one video | Use an evidence cutaway at 04:12 because the speaker names the document | Revision-bound editorial decision |
| Technique catalog | Reusable vocabulary, inputs, ranges, conflicts, and evidence | Split edit, reaction hold, music drop | Descriptive capability/reference data |

Universal rules should remain few and durable. Creative taste should not be hardened into
universal policy, and a heuristic should never become a quota merely because it can be
counted.

## Proposed decision rules

### D01 — Begin with the editorial goal

Every salient intervention should name the problem it is solving: clarity, evidence,
continuity, emphasis, emotional timing, humor, compression, orientation, coherent brand
identity or atmosphere, or another
explicit goal. “Increase engagement” is too vague by itself.

### D02 — Include the no-effect alternative

A proposal must consider leaving the beat alone. The selected technique should add more
value than the unmodified performance, not merely add activity.

### D03 — Anchor decisions to content

Select a source range and cite a transcript phrase, visual event, narrative beat, or
continuity need. Do not place effects by elapsed-time schedules such as “every four
seconds” or “three times per minute.”

### D04 — Spend contrast deliberately

An intervention is salient because the surrounding material differs. Avoid stacking
motion, dense text, music changes, sound effects, and color treatment on the same beat
unless each layer is necessary and the combination remains intelligible.

### D05 — Preserve useful air

Silence, breaths, reaction holds, and unaltered framing are valid techniques. Remove a
pause only after deciding it is redundant rather than thoughtful, emotional, comic, or
needed for comprehension. Evaluate characteristic phrasing, comic repetition, rhetorical
questions, and self-corrections for their contribution to the speaker's personality before
polishing them away.

### D06 — Make supporting imagery evidentiary

Prefer B-roll, screenshots, graphics, and source excerpts that prove, explain, locate, or
contrast the spoken point. Displaying a source is not verification: distinguish its claims
from the presenter's inference, opinion, or joke, and retain material attribution and
qualifications. Decorative imagery can support identity or atmosphere but must not imply
an event, person, product, or causal relationship that the source does not support.

### D07 — Protect comprehension and accessibility

Speech intelligibility takes precedence over music and sound design. Text must be readable
for its actual on-screen duration. Meaning cannot depend on color, stereo position, or an
audio-only cue. Caption timing follows the rendered edit, not the pre-edit transcript.
Enlarge the relevant source passage instead of expecting a nested webpage to remain
legible. Check that host insets, captions, decorative frames, and platform UI do not cover
essential material; preserve enough context to avoid a misleading crop.

### D08 — Treat density and frequency as contextual

The system may estimate intervention density and warn about clusters, but it must not use
a universal cut, zoom, caption, or pattern-interrupt interval. Defaults in a style profile
are soft proposal constraints and require an explanation when they are exceeded.

### D09 — Learn taste only through explicit feedback

Accepted, changed, rejected, and deferred proposals become revision-bound feedback
events. A durable profile changes only after owner confirmation; the system does not infer
a permanent preference from one acceptance or from retention analytics alone.

### D10 — Interpret analytics as diagnostic, not causal proof

Retention dips, spikes, and top moments are prompts for inspection. A spike can reflect
delight or confusion; a dip can reflect an edit, content, audience mismatch, or an earlier
broken promise. Compare like-for-like videos and record alternative explanations.

### D10a — Keep platform and surface evidence in scope

Record platform, surface, format, aspect ratio, duration, audience, and objective with a
style profile. A short-form swipe-feed or advertising convention may inform an experiment,
but it does not become a long-form YouTube default without a stated transfer rationale and
project-specific evidence. “Stop the scroll” and “sustain a chosen long-form view” are
related but different editorial problems.

### D11 — Extract principles from references, not signatures

Reference videos may demonstrate a vocabulary or relationship—such as a tonal treatment
marking an aside—but should not be copied beat-for-beat or converted into a creator-wide
quota. Preserve the owner’s voice and disclose uncertainty about what a reference edit was
intended to accomplish.

### D12 — Review the actual audiovisual result

Metadata, render success, waveform statistics, and pixel checks are supporting evidence,
not creative acceptance. Listen at normal speed, inspect representative frames and
transitions, compare against the source, and record the disposition under the existing
quality workflow.

### D13 — Treat title and thumbnail as one promise

Assign distinct jobs to the pair and audit the opening against both. The title may carry
verified entities, topic language, and event context; the thumbnail may carry the immediate
visual subject, channel recognition, and a short complementary interpretation. Duplication
is allowed only when it materially improves clarity.

### D14 — Separate stable brand tokens from variable story content

Version a small recognition system—type roles, anchor zones, creator/logo treatment,
accent behavior, and compositional rhythm—while reserving most of the image for the
specific video. Consistency is a tool for recognition, not a reason to make every subject
look the same. Extend appropriate tokens into the video: host framing, source windows,
text treatments, and transitions can share an identity without adding constant decoration.
Readability and the current story take precedence over ornamental consistency.

### D15 — Declare search, browse, and subscriber intent

A searchable title should clearly describe the topic in language that can match relevant
queries. A browse-oriented title may lean further into truthful tension or curiosity. The
chosen surface and audience must be recorded; Studio must not call keyword density alone
“optimization.”

### D16 — Test the actual small-format package

Review each variant at representative Home, Suggested, Subscription, and Search sizes,
both alone and in a channel-grid/browse-wall context. Verify spelling, hierarchy, identity,
provenance, policy, title-thumbnail complement, and the intro payoff.

### D17 — Version YouTube experiment behavior

YouTube currently supports up to three variants for title-only, thumbnail-only, or joint
title-thumbnail tests and selects by watch time. Record the test mode, hypotheses, platform
contract version, result label, audience context, and uncertainty. “Performed same” and
“inconclusive” are not winners, and CTR alone is not the native test objective.

### D18 — Let the current beat determine visual hierarchy

Choose host-led commentary, source-dominant inspection, or a shared source/reaction
layout according to what deserves attention. A visible host inset earns its space through
reaction or continuity; it is not mandatory. Switch at meaningful content boundaries,
not fixed intervals, and reconsider the layout for each delivery format.

## Approved reference lessons — September 9, 2026

Reference: [voidzilla — hunter biden is launching a memecoin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=56SN_91gitM).
The case review used the auto-transcript and sampled frames across the video, not
continuous audiovisual playback. Observations include an immediate topic-led opening,
host/source/shared layouts, an enlarged highlighted article passage around 4:05, a
source-dominant distribution chart around 6:08, and recurring computer-window framing.
Conversational phrasing and jokes were evident in the transcript; audio mix, vocal
delivery, precise cut frequency, and causal retention benefits were not established.

The owner approved adapting the general principles into R07, R15, R19, and R32–R34:
preserve personality, assign attention deliberately, present sources readably and honestly,
and recognize branding/atmosphere as legitimate editorial purposes. This is
`case-observation` plus owner-approved policy, not measured effectiveness or platform
consensus. It does not prescribe the creator's retro aesthetic, frequent effects, or a
universal long-form/Shorts/TikTok style. Concrete reusable Studio layout controls remain
implementation work, not a capability proven by these rules.

## Proposal record

Each Studio proposal should carry:

- `technique_id` and catalog version;
- editorial goal and semantic trigger;
- source and output time ranges;
- proposed parameters and asset provenance;
- alternatives, including no change;
- accessibility, continuity, and truthfulness risks;
- confidence and evidence category;
- owner disposition and revision binding.

## Evidence categories

- `measured`: supported by a directly relevant experiment or quantitative synthesis;
- `platform-guidance`: stated by YouTube or an accessibility authority;
- `practice-consensus`: repeated across credible professional practice, without a clean
  causal estimate;
- `case-observation`: directly observed in one or more reference edits;
- `plausible`: supported by a reasonable perceptual or narrative mechanism;
- `exploratory`: worth testing, but presently weak, conflicting, or highly contextual.

The categories describe the claim, not the technique forever. A technique can have strong
support for comprehension in one context and only exploratory support for retention in
another.

## Anti-patterns

- effects on a timer;
- automatic deletion of every silence or filler word;
- captions that duplicate dense graphics and compete with the speaker;
- B-roll chosen by keyword alone without checking what it implies;
- music used to manufacture emotion the content has not earned;
- “retention editing” presented as a guaranteed algorithmic advantage;
- analytics-driven profile mutation without explicit owner review;
- treating an admired creator’s current style as a universal house style.
- freezing a recognizable thumbnail template so tightly that story-specific imagery has no room;
- testing three cosmetic variants that do not answer different packaging questions;
- optimizing title keywords without checking whether the wording remains human, accurate, and front-loaded;
- assuming a click proves the title-thumbnail promise was satisfied.
